STRATEGY: Senior Health Workforce Analyst — Pre-Interview Assignment
Task: 15-minute technical presentation on forecasting workforce supply and
requirements for a regulated health profession in Ontario over 10 years.

================================================================================
0. CHOSEN PROFESSION & FRAMING
================================================================================
- Pick one profession with available public data and clear policy relevance,
  e.g. Registered Nurses (RNs) or Family Physicians. Recommendation: RNs,
  because CIHI, CNO (College of Nurses of Ontario) and Statistics Canada all
  publish usable annual data, and nursing shortages are a current Ontario
  policy priority (easy to tie to "government action").
- Frame the deck around one central question: "Will Ontario's supply of RNs
  meet projected population health service needs by 2036, overall and by
  region, and what levers close any gap?"

================================================================================
1. ANALYTICAL & POLICY QUESTIONS (Slide section a)
================================================================================
- Supply side: how many licensed, *active, practicing* RNs will Ontario have
  each year (stock-and-flow, not just headcount of registrants).
- Requirements side: how many RN FTEs are needed to deliver an adequate level
  of service given demographic change (aging population, population growth)
  and evolving care models (shift to community/home care).
- Distribution: supply and requirements modelled by LHIN/Ontario Health
  region and by urban/rural/north to surface maldistribution, not just a
  province-wide gap.
- Core policy questions: Where/when do gaps emerge? Which levers (seat
  expansions, internationally educated nurse (IEN) licensing, retention,
  scope-of-practice changes, rural incentives) close them fastest and most
  cost-effectively?

================================================================================
2. MODELLING METHODOLOGY (section b)
================================================================================
Two linked components, presented as a pipeline:

  (1) STOCK-AND-FLOW SUPPLY MODEL (system dynamics / cohort projection)
      Supply(t+1) = Supply(t) + New entrants(t) - Attrition(t) + Net migration(t)
      - New entrants: nursing program graduates + IEN licensure, lagged by
        program length and licensing exam pass rates.
      - Attrition: age/career-stage-specific retirement and exit rates
        (apply age-cohort hazard rates rather than one flat attrition %).
      - Net migration: interprovincial/international flows from CIHI/CNO data.
      - Run per region to capture internal migration between regions.

  (2) REQUIREMENTS / DEMAND MODEL (needs-based, population health approach)
      Requirement(t) = Population(t) x utilization rate x service-intensity
                        adjustment, decomposed by age band and care setting.
      - Population projections by region/age from Ontario Ministry of
        Finance population projections.
      - Utilization/intensity rates from health service use data, adjusted
        for policy-driven changes (e.g. more home care, skill-mix changes).
      - Alternative simpler lens to mention: ratio-based benchmarking
        (RNs per 1,000 population) as a sensitivity check against the
        needs-based estimate.

  (3) OPTIMIZATION LAYER (the differentiator — explicitly called out)
      - Once supply and requirement trajectories are produced per region,
        formulate a constrained optimization model to recommend how to close
        projected gaps at least cost / within budget:
          Decision variables: number of new domestic training seats to fund,
          number of IEN licenses to fast-track, retention-incentive spend,
          and inter-regional deployment incentives, each year.
          Objective: minimize (total policy cost) subject to closing the
          projected supply-requirement gap in every region and year, or
          minimize the maximum remaining gap subject to a fixed budget
          (equivalent formulations — present both and pick one for the demo).
          Constraints: training pipeline capacity (seats, clinical placement
          capacity), annual budget ceilings, realistic ramp-up rates,
          minimum service levels per region.
      - This reframes the exercise from "descriptive forecast" to decision
        support: the model doesn't just predict a gap, it recommends the
        policy mix that closes it most efficiently.
      - Keep the math simple enough to present in 2-3 slides: a linear or
        mixed-integer program (LP/MIP) solved with open-source tooling
        (e.g. Python + PuLP/OR-Tools) is sufficient and easy to explain to a
        non-technical audience; avoid overengineering for a 15-minute talk.

================================================================================
3. DATA SOURCES, VARIABLES, DATA QUALITY & PRIVACY (section c)
================================================================================
- Supply-side data: CNO annual registration statistics (counts by age,
  region, class of registration), CIHI Nursing in Canada reports, National
  Nursing Database.
- Demand-side data: Ontario Ministry of Finance population projections,
  CIHI health service utilization data, Ontario Health regional reports,
  StatCan health workforce surveys.
- Key variables: headcount vs FTE, age distribution, region/LHIN, practice
  setting, full-time/part-time status, graduation and licensing rates.
- Data quality issues to flag: registration counts overstate "active
  practicing" supply; regional data can be sparse/suppressed for small
  cells; lag between data collection and publication (typically 1-2 years);
  self-reported hours worked can be unreliable.
- Privacy: all data used should be aggregate/de-identified, consistent with
  CIHI/CNO public data use agreements; no individual-level licensing or
  patient data required; follow Ontario's FIPPA principles for any
  internal/administrative data accessed.

================================================================================
4. UNCERTAINTY, SCENARIOS & SENSITIVITY (section d)
================================================================================
- Treat this as a monte carlo / scenario exercise layered on the
  stock-and-flow model, not a single point forecast.
- Scenarios: baseline (status quo trends), high-attrition (accelerated
  retirements), expanded-seats (policy intervention), high-immigration/IEN
  fast-tracking.
- Sensitivity analysis on key uncertain parameters: attrition rate,
  utilization rate growth, program completion/pass rates, migration rates —
  tornado chart showing which assumptions move the gap estimate most.
- For the optimization layer: solve under each scenario to show how the
  recommended policy mix and cost change with the underlying uncertainty
  (robust/stress-tested recommendation rather than a single "optimal" plan).

================================================================================
5. COMMUNICATING RESULTS & POLICY IMPLICATIONS (section e)
================================================================================
- Lead with a single headline chart: projected supply vs. requirement,
  province-wide and by region, 2026-2036, with a shaded uncertainty band.
- Follow with the optimization result: "the lowest-cost policy mix that
  closes the gap" as a simple bar/stacked chart (seats vs IEN licensing vs
  retention spend vs year).
- State limitations plainly: model quality depends on data lag and
  assumption validity; does not capture non-linear system shocks (e.g.
  pandemics); regional model is coarser than facility-level planning.
- Policy implications framed as options with trade-offs (cost, lead time,
  political feasibility), not a single recommendation — supports
  evidence-informed decision-making by senior management/government.

================================================================================
6. WEBAPP / DEMO COMPONENT (extra deliverable beyond the core ask)
================================================================================
Goal: a lightweight, interactive artifact to show during/after the
presentation that makes the model tangible, without overengineering.

- Stack: Python backend (model logic reused directly from the
  stock-and-flow + optimization scripts) + a simple interactive front end.
  Recommended for a quick build: Streamlit (fastest path to an interactive
  dashboard with sliders/scenario pickers, no separate frontend needed).
  Alternative if a "real" web app is wanted: FastAPI backend + React/Vite
  frontend with Plotly/Recharts charts — more effort, only pursue if time
  allows.
- Core screens:
  1. Scenario control panel: sliders/dropdowns for attrition rate,
     training seat growth, IEN licensing rate, region selector.
  2. Supply vs requirement chart (province + per-region) updating live as
     scenario inputs change.
  3. "Optimizer" panel: given a budget slider, show the recommended policy
     mix (seats/IEN/retention spend by year) and resulting gap closure,
     calling the LP/MIP solver on the fly (PuLP or OR-Tools).
  4. Downloadable summary table/CSV of projected gaps and recommended
     interventions.
- Keep the optimization model solvable in well under a second for a smooth
  interactive demo (small LP, annual time steps, handful of regions).
- Use this app as a live demo in the last 1-2 minutes of the presentation,
  or leave it as a leave-behind artifact/link for the interview panel.

================================================================================
7. PREPARATION PLAN / TIMELINE
================================================================================
1. Finalize profession choice and gather 2-3 key public datasets (CNO/CIHI/
   StatCan/Ontario population projections).
2. Build the stock-and-flow supply model and the needs-based requirement
   model in Python (pandas) using real or reasonably approximated data.
3. Layer the optimization model (PuLP/OR-Tools) on top to generate the
   recommended policy mix under a baseline scenario.
4. Run 3-4 scenarios and sensitivity checks; capture the headline charts.
5. Build the Streamlit demo app wrapping the same model code.
6. Draft the 15-minute slide deck following sections a-e above, each with
   1-3 slides; reserve the last slides for the optimizer + webapp demo and
   explicit "limitations" slide.
7. Rehearse for time (aim ~12-13 minutes content + 2-3 minutes buffer/demo).
